> [!note] Key ideas
> - A Unit Hydrograph (UH) of duration $D$ is defined as the Direct Runoff Hydrograph resulting from $1	ext{ cm}$ (or $1	ext{ mm}$) of uniform rainfall excess generated uniformly over the entire watershed at an independent constant rate over duration $D$.
> - Two foundational assumptions: **Linear response (Proportionality)** — doubling excess rainfall doubles DRH ordinates; **Time invariance (Superposition)** — identical storm inputs produce identical responses regardless of when they occur.
> - To derive a $D$-hour UH from an observed storm: isolate single-peaked event, separate baseflow, integrate DRH to find runoff depth $R$ (cm), and divide all DRH ordinates by $R$: $U_i = Q_{d,i} / R$.

## 1. Concept and Definition of the Unit Hydrograph

Formulated by **Leroy K. Sherman in 1932**, the Unit Hydrograph (UH) remains the cornerstone of engineering hydrology for predicting catchment runoff from design storms.

```
   Excess Rainfall                   Direct Runoff Hydrograph (DRH)
   +-------------+                   ^
   | 1 cm excess |                   |        Peak U_p = Q_p / R
   | duration D  |    =======>       |            /   +-------------+                   |           /                                       |          /                                         |         /                                           +--------+--------+--------> Time
                                              |<-- tp ->|
                                              |<------ tb ------>|
```

### Fundamental Hypotheses

1. **Linearity (Proportionality):** The ordinates of direct runoff are directly proportional to the total volume of rainfall excess. If $R	ext{ cm}$ of rainfall excess occurs in duration $D$, each ordinate of the resulting DRH is $R$ times the corresponding ordinate of the $D$-hour unit hydrograph:
$$Q_d(t) = R \cdot U(t)$$
2. **Time Invariance:** The direct runoff response from a given rainfall excess is invariant with respect to time. A storm of duration $D$ occurring today produces the exact same hydrograph shape as the same storm occurring next month.
3. **Linear Superposition:** The total direct runoff response to successive blocks of rainfall excess is the algebraic sum of the direct runoff hydrographs produced by each individual block lagged appropriately in time.

---

## 2. Derivation Procedure from an Isolated Storm

To extract a $D$-hour unit hydrograph from streamflow observations:

1. **Storm Selection:** Choose an intense, isolated, uniform single-peaked storm whose effective duration is approximately $D$.
2. **Baseflow Separation:** Deduce baseflow $Q_b(t)$ from total streamflow to obtain direct runoff hydrograph ordinates:
$$Q_{d,i} = Q_{total,i} - Q_{b,i}$$
3. **Determine Runoff Depth ($R$):**
$$V_d = \Delta t \sum_{i=1}^{n} Q_{d,i}$$
$$R = rac{V_d}{A} 	imes 100	ext{ (in cm)}$$
4. **Compute Unit Hydrograph Ordinates ($U_i$):**
$$U_i = rac{Q_{d,i}}{R}	ext{ (m}^3	ext{/s per cm)}$$
5. **Verify UH Depth:**
$$rac{\Delta t \sum U_i}{A} = 1.0	ext{ cm}$$

## 3. Worked Numerical Examples

### Worked Example 21.1: Derivation of a 6-hour Unit Hydrograph

**Problem:** A $250	ext{ km}^2$ catchment receives an isolated 6-hour storm yielding the direct runoff hydrograph below. Derive the 6-hour unit hydrograph ordinates.

| Time (h) | 0 | 6 | 12 | 18 | 24 | 30 | 36 | 42 | 48 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DRH ($Q_d$, $	ext{m}^3	ext{/s}$) | 0 | 28 | 70 | 105 | 84 | 49 | 21 | 7 | 0 |

**Solution:**

**Step 1: Sum DRH ordinates**
$$\sum Q_d = 0 + 28 + 70 + 105 + 84 + 49 + 21 + 7 + 0 = 364	ext{ m}^3	ext{/s}$$

**Step 2: Calculate total direct runoff volume**
$$\Delta t = 6	ext{ h} = 21{,}600	ext{ s}$$
$$V_d = 364 	imes 21{,}600 = 7{,}862{,}400	ext{ m}^3$$

**Step 3: Calculate effective rainfall depth ($R$)**
$$R = rac{7{,}862{,}400	ext{ m}^3}{250 	imes 10^6	ext{ m}^2} = 0.03145	ext{ m} = 3.145	ext{ cm} pprox 3.5	ext{ cm (or direct exact value)}$$
Using $R = 3.145	ext{ cm}$:

**Step 4: Compute UH ordinates ($U_i = Q_{d,i} / R$)**
- $t = 0$: $0 / 3.145 = 0	ext{ m}^3	ext{/s}$
- $t = 6$: $28 / 3.145 = 8.90	ext{ m}^3	ext{/s}$
- $t = 12$: $70 / 3.145 = 22.26	ext{ m}^3	ext{/s}$
- $t = 18$: $105 / 3.145 = 33.39	ext{ m}^3	ext{/s}$
- $t = 24$: $84 / 3.145 = 26.71	ext{ m}^3	ext{/s}$
- $t = 30$: $49 / 3.145 = 15.58	ext{ m}^3	ext{/s}$
- $t = 36$: $21 / 3.145 = 6.68	ext{ m}^3	ext{/s}$
- $t = 42$: $7 / 3.145 = 2.23	ext{ m}^3	ext{/s}$
- $t = 48$: $0	ext{ m}^3	ext{/s}$

### Session 21 recap
1. A Unit Hydrograph represents the direct runoff from $1	ext{ cm}$ of rainfall excess.
2. Sherman's assumptions: linearity, time invariance, and uniform areal distribution.
3. Derivation: $U_i = Q_{d,i} / R$, where $R = (\Delta t \sum Q_{d,i}) / A$.
4. Check condition: $\Delta t \sum U_i = 1	ext{ cm} 	imes A$.

:::exam[10-Mark Numerical]
The ordinates of a 4-hour unit hydrograph are given below:

| Time (h) | 0 | 4 | 8 | 12 | 16 | 20 | 24 | 28 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| UH ($	ext{m}^3	ext{/s}$) | 0 | 12 | 35 | 55 | 40 | 22 | 8 | 0 |

A storm produces successive 4-hour rainfall excesses of $2.0	ext{ cm}$ and $3.0	ext{ cm}$. Assuming a constant baseflow of $10	ext{ m}^3	ext{/s}$, compute the resulting peak streamflow and the time of its occurrence.
:::

:::sol
By convolution:
- Block 1 ($R_1 = 2.0	ext{ cm}$ at $t = 0$): $Q_{d1} = 2.0 	imes U(t)$
- Block 2 ($R_2 = 3.0	ext{ cm}$ at $t = 4$): $Q_{d2} = 3.0 	imes U(t-4)$
At $t = 12	ext{ h}$:
$Q_{d}(12) = 2.0 	imes U(12) + 3.0 	imes U(8) = 2.0(55) + 3.0(35) = 110 + 105 = 215	ext{ m}^3	ext{/s}$.
At $t = 16	ext{ h}$:
$Q_{d}(16) = 2.0 	imes U(16) + 3.0 	imes U(12) = 2.0(40) + 3.0(55) = 80 + 165 = 245	ext{ m}^3	ext{/s}$.
At $t = 20	ext{ h}$:
$Q_{d}(20) = 2.0 	imes U(20) + 3.0 	imes U(16) = 2.0(22) + 3.0(40) = 44 + 120 = 164	ext{ m}^3	ext{/s}$.
Peak direct runoff $= 245	ext{ m}^3	ext{/s}$ at $t = 16	ext{ h}$.
Total peak streamflow $= 245 + 10 = 255	ext{ m}^3	ext{/s}$ at $t = 16	ext{ hours}$.
:::
