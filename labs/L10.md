**Goal:** delineate a real catchment from a DEM inside HEC-HMS — the starting model for your PBL project.

**You need:** QGIS and HEC-HMS; a free account on **Bhuvan** (CartoDEM) or **USGS EarthExplorer** (SRTM 1 arc-second). **Goes with:** Session 9 (instructor demonstration in class; each PBL group then repeats it for its own site). **Time:** about 60 minutes.

> [!note] Before you start
> Try the D8 grid in Session 9 (Theory tab): every step below is what that grid does, on millions of cells.

<!--step: Download a DEM-->
1. Decide the outlet of your PBL catchment and draw a generous box around the whole catchment (at least 2–3 km beyond the divide).
2. Download a **30 m DEM** covering that box: **CartoDEM** from Bhuvan (W5) or **SRTM 1 arc-second** from USGS EarthExplorer. Note the source and date for your report.

> [!fix] The catchment crosses two DEM tiles
> Download both and merge them in QGIS: <span class="menu">Raster › Miscellaneous › Merge</span>.

<!--step: Clip and reproject the DEM in QGIS-->
1. Load the DEM in QGIS.
2. <span class="menu">Raster › Extraction › Clip Raster by Extent</span> to your box.
3. <span class="menu">Raster › Projections › Warp (Reproject)</span> to **EPSG:32643**, output as **GeoTIFF**, e.g. `dem_utm.tif`.

> [!see] You should see
> A raster with values in metres (roughly 700–950 m around Bengaluru) and a pixel size of about 30 m in the layer properties.

<!--step: Create the PBL project and load terrain-->
1. In HEC-HMS: <span class="menu">File › New</span>, name `PBL_<Group>`, **Metric**.
2. <span class="menu">Components › Terrain Data Manager</span> › **New…**: choose `dem_utm.tif`, vertical units **Meters**.

<!--step: Create a basin model that uses the terrain-->
1. Create a basin model, e.g. `PBL_Basin`.
2. In its Component Editor set **Terrain Data** to the DEM you just added. If asked, set the coordinate system (<span class="menu">GIS › Coordinate System</span>) to the DEM's projection.

> [!see] You should see
> The DEM drawn on the basin-model map as a shaded background.

<!--step: Fill sinks and compute drainage-->
1. <span class="menu">GIS › Preprocess Sinks</span> — the "fill sinks" step.
2. <span class="menu">GIS › Preprocess Drainage</span> — flow direction and flow accumulation (D8).

<!--step: Identify streams-->
<span class="menu">GIS › Identify Streams</span>. Enter a **threshold area**: start with about 1–2 % of your catchment area (e.g. 1 km² for a 50 km² catchment).

> [!hms] Choosing the threshold
> Too small → a dense web of tiny channels and many tiny subbasins; too large → missing tributaries. Compare the streams with the OpenStreetMap or Survey of India drainage and adjust.

<!--step: Place the outlet (break point)-->
Select the **Break Point Creation Tool** and click on the stream **at your outlet** — and at any internal point where you need a subbasin boundary (a tank, a gauging station, a road culvert).

> [!fix] The break point is beside the stream
> Zoom in until the stream cells are visible and click exactly on one; a point off the stream delineates a tiny catchment.

<!--step: Delineate elements-->
<span class="menu">GIS › Delineate Elements</span>. HEC-HMS creates subbasins, reaches and junctions upstream of your break point(s), with their areas filled in.

<!--step: Compute characteristics and check-->
1. Use the GIS / Parameters menu option to compute **characteristics** (longest flow path, slopes) for the elements.
2. Compare the delineated area with the area you expected (map, India-WRIS, or a published figure).

> [!warn] Flat cities fool DEMs
> In urban areas, drains, culverts and road embankments control where water goes and a 30 m DEM cannot see them. Check the boundary against drainage maps and a site visit, and say in your report how you checked it.

<!--end-->
