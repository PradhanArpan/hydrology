**Goal:** read hyetographs and cumulative (mass) curves out of HEC-HMS, export them, and check them against your hand analysis.

**You need:** `Run_Thiessen` (L05) and a spreadsheet. **Goes with:** Session 7. **Time:** about 30 minutes.

<!--step: The gauge hyetograph-->
Click `TG1` › its date node › **Graph**. Compare with the hyetograph you drew in Session 7.

<!--step: Enter a gauge as a cumulative series-->
1. Copy `TG1` to `TG1_cum`. In its Time-Series Gage tab change **Units** to **Cumulative Millimeters**.
2. In its Table enter the cumulative values from the CSV: 2, 6, 13, 25, 43, 69, 83, 92, 98, 102, 104, 105.
3. Open its Graph.

> [!see] You should see
> HEC-HMS converts the mass curve back into the same incremental hyetograph. A recording gauge's chart *is* a mass curve; the model stores the increments.

<!--step: Subbasin precipitation from the results-->
1. **Results** › `Run_Thiessen` › `Sub1` › **Time-Series Table**.
2. Find the precipitation column (and, if shown, excess precipitation and outflow).

> [!see] You should see
> 15-minute values: each 30-minute TG1 increment split in two, scaled so the total is the Thiessen depth (102.8 mm instead of 105).

<!--step: Export to a spreadsheet-->
1. In the time-series table select the rows (click the first cell, <kbd>Shift</kbd>-click the last) and copy with <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>C</kbd>.
2. Paste into a spreadsheet. (Some versions also offer <span class="menu">File › Print / Export</span> or a right-click *Copy*.)
3. Add a cumulative column and plot the **mass curve** of the subbasin rainfall.

<!--step: Check the maximum intensities-->
From the exported subbasin series, find the maximum 1-hour and 2-hour depths with a sliding window (`=MAX()` over a column of 4- and 8-interval sums).

> [!see] You should see
> Values ≈ 0.979 × the TG1 values from Session 7 (43.1 mm in 1 h and 68.5 mm in 2 h), because the subbasin depth is 102.8/105 = 0.979 of TG1.

<!--step: Change the computation interval-->
1. Change `Day1`'s time interval to **5 minutes** (or make a copy, `Day1_5min`) and re-run.
2. Look at the subbasin hyetograph again.

> [!hms] What to notice
> HEC-HMS divides each 30-minute bar into equal 5-minute slices; it cannot invent detail the gauge did not record. For a small urban catchment that responds in 15–30 minutes, daily or even hourly rainfall cannot show the real peak burst — the reason for recording gauges and the 10 % rule.

<!--end-->
