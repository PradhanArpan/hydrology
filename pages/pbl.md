## Driving question

> **How much runoff will our catchment generate in a design storm, and how much of it can be safely conveyed, stored or recharged?**

Each group of 4–5 students adopts one real catchment or site and answers this question through four milestones that follow the units of the course. The same HEC-HMS model grows through all four milestones.

## Milestones

| Milestone | Weeks | Content | Model state at the end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **M1 Rainfall analysis** | 3–5 | Catchment delineation; station data (IMD/KSNDMC); consistency (double mass curve); missing data; mean areal rainfall (three methods); IDF relation | HMS project with delineated subbasins, gauges and a meteorologic model |
| **M2 Losses and runoff** | 6–8 | Evaporation, infiltration/ET and runoff estimates; land use and CN; rainfall-runoff relation | Event model with loss methods; runoff volume checked by hand |
| **M3 Hydrograph and flood** | 9–12 | Base flow; unit hydrograph (derived or synthetic); S-curve; design flood; rating curve or routing | Calibrated or justified transform; design-storm run; routing where relevant |
| **M4 Solution and presentation** | 13–15 | Harvesting / recharge / drainage proposal sized from the analysis; report, poster, presentation | Scenario runs comparing the proposal with the present condition |

## Rules that apply to every milestone

- **Data citation.** Every dataset must be cited with its source (IMD, India-WRIS, KSNDMC, CGWB or field measurement) and download date.
- **Hand check.** Every number taken from HEC-HMS or HEC-RAS must be checked against at least one hand or spreadsheet calculation in your workbook. A model result with no independent check earns no marks for that item.
- **AI-use log.** If you use an AI tool, log the prompt, the output, how you verified it, what you corrected, and what you learned. Unverified AI output is not accepted as engineering reasoning.
- **Model files.** Submit the zipped HMS project folder, not only screenshots.
